What Is a Scheduled Exam?

The core difference between Manual and Scheduled, in plain terms

Every exam in this portal runs in one of two modes, chosen with the Scheduled Exam toggle when you create or edit it:

Manual Exam — you control when it opens by changing its Status yourself (Upcoming → Ongoing → Completed) on Manage Exams. Nothing happens automatically; a Manual Exam only becomes startable the moment you set it to Ongoing, no matter what date/time is stored on it.

Scheduled Exam — you set a date, a start time and a duration once, and the portal computes everything else on its own. There is no Status dropdown to manage: the exam automatically becomes Live at the exact configured second and automatically becomes Completed once its time is up. You never touch it again after creating it.

Manual Exam
"I want to open this exam myself, whenever I decide the moment is right."
  • You flip the Status field to Ongoing yourself
  • Students can never start it until you do — even past its stored date/time
  • You flip it to Completed yourself too, whenever you choose
  • No preparation window, no auto-submit deadline enforcement, no buffer
Scheduled Exam
"I want the exam to become available automatically at a specific date/time, with no manual step from me."
  • Status is computed from the clock — Upcoming / Ongoing / Completed, always in sync with real time
  • Optional preparation window lets students preload before the exact start second
  • Deadline is enforced by the server itself, even if the student's browser is closed
  • Optional completion buffer absorbs slow/late submissions right at the deadline
Rule of thumb: if you plan to personally watch the clock and click a button to open the exam, use Manual. If you want to set a date/time once and walk away, use Scheduled.

How to Create a Scheduled Exam

Step by step, using the exact fields on Manage Exams → Add Exam

  1. Fill in the standard fieldsExam Name, Date, Start Time, Duration (min), Total Questions, Category and Subcategory — the same fields every exam needs, Manual or Scheduled.
  2. Turn on "Scheduled Exam"This single toggle switches the form: the Status dropdown disappears (it's computed automatically from here on) and three new fields appear — Preparation Window, Completion Buffer, and Allow Manual Submission.
  3. Set Preparation Window and Completion BufferBoth are optional minute values, blank meaning 0. See sections 3 and 5 below for exactly what each one does.
  4. Decide Allow Manual SubmissionOn (the default) lets students submit early; off hides the Submit button and relies entirely on the deadline. See section 6.
  5. Fill in the rest as usualInstructions, Positive/Negative Marks, Max Attempts, Passing Percentage, and Result Mode — unchanged from a Manual Exam.
  6. Add questions afterwardSame as any exam — Manage Questions, using whichever method you'd normally use.
The form does not stop you from picking a date/time that's already in the past — nothing validates that Date/Start Time is in the future. If you save one by mistake, the exam will immediately compute as Ongoing or even Completed the moment you save it, since its effective status is derived purely from the clock. Double-check the date and time before saving.

Preparation / Preload Window

Why it exists, and exactly what a student can and can't do during it

Why it exists: when a student clicks Start, the portal has to fetch every question and resolve every question image before the exam can open — for an image-heavy exam this takes real time (each image is a lookup against object storage). Doing that fetch before the official start, while the student waits anyway, means the exam opens instantly the moment it actually goes live instead of showing a loading screen at the worst possible moment.

Open the instructions page, click Prepare, have their questions/images loaded in the background, and watch the live countdown to the official start.

During the 3 minutes, a student CANNOT

See any question content, answer anything, or have an attempt recorded — the attempt itself is only created once the official start second is confirmed by the server.

Refresh / reopen during preparation: completely safe. The portal remembers server-side (in that student's own session) that they already clicked Prepare — reopening or refreshing the instructions page skips straight back into the same countdown (or launches immediately, if the official start already passed while they were away) instead of making them click Prepare again.
4

Start Time + Duration + End Time

There is no separate "end time" field — it's always derived

Start Time + Duration = Official End (the deadline)

You never configure an end time directly. 2:00 PM + 30 minutes = 2:30 PM — that instant is both when the exam's official window closes (no new attempt can start after it) and the latest possible moment any attempt's personal timer can run to.

A late start never extends the exam. Every attempt's real deadline is min(attempt start + Duration, official end) — whichever comes first. A student who starts late loses time; they never gain any.

Example — on-time start: exam starts 2:00 PM, Duration 30 min. A student who starts right at 2:00 PM gets the full 30 minutes, deadline 2:30 PM.

Example — late start: same exam, but a student only clicks Start at 2:10 PM (10 minutes late — still within the official window, which stays open until 2:30 PM). Their personal timer would normally run 30 minutes to 2:40 PM, but the deadline is capped at the official end — so their real deadline is still 2:30 PM. They effectively get 20 minutes, not 30.

Example — too late: a student who doesn't click Start until 2:31 PM can't start at all — the official window (start → official end) has already closed. The Start button on their instructions page is no longer enabled.

Buffer / Completion Window

A safety margin for the server, not extra time for the student

Why it exists: right at the deadline, some students' submissions are already "in flight" — a slow connection, a throttled background browser tab, a moment of lag. Without any buffer, that submission could arrive a few seconds after the deadline and be rejected outright. The Completion Buffer gives the server a short grace period to still accept and score submissions that were already underway.

What it does not do: it does not add time to any student's timer. A student's own countdown still reaches zero exactly at the official end (Start + Duration) — the buffer only affects whether the server is still willing to accept the resulting submission, not how long they're allowed to keep answering.

When to use each: leave it On for practice tests or anything where finishing early is a convenience worth offering. Turn it Off for exams where every student must use exactly the same amount of time — nobody can "bank" extra time by submitting early and comparing notes.

What actually happens at the deadline is not a browser timer. The server runs its own background process that continuously scans for attempts whose deadline has passed, claims each one exactly once (so it's never scored twice even under heavy concurrent load), and finalizes it from the student's answers — which are saved to the database periodically while they work, not just at the end. This means an attempt is still correctly finalized even if the student's browser crashes, loses connection, or is closed outright; nothing needs to be "open" for the deadline to be enforced.
Even when submission is Allowed, the same server-side deadline enforcement still runs underneath — if a student simply lets the timer run out instead of clicking Submit, they're finalized exactly the same way. And if submission is Disabled, the server also actively rejects a manual submit request sent before the deadline (not just hides the button) — bypassing the UI doesn't bypass the rule.

Cancel / Restore

The Cancel button on Manage Exams, and what it actually toggles

What Cancel does: marks the exam with an explicit Cancelled status. From that moment:

  • The exam disappears entirely from every student's dashboard (all three tabs) and from the notification popup — it isn't shown as "cancelled" to students, it simply isn't shown.
  • No new attempt can be started by anyone, effective immediately.
  • On Manage Exams it shows a Cancelled badge so you can find it again.

What Cancel does not do: a student who is already mid-attempt when you cancel is not interrupted — their attempt keeps running against its own stored deadline exactly as before, and is still auto-finalized normally when that deadline arrives. Cancelling only blocks new attempts; it does not reach into one already in progress.

Restoring a cancelled exam does not pause its clock. Restore simply removes the Cancelled marker — the original date/time/duration are untouched, so the exam's effective status is immediately recomputed from real time. If you cancel an exam and restore it a day later, and its original scheduled window has already passed, it will come back as Ongoing (if still inside its buffer) or straight to Completed — not back to Upcoming.

Practical example — "This exam is scheduled for tomorrow, but I've just realized two of the questions are wrong."

Recommended action: Cancel it now (safe — it's still Upcoming, no attempts exist yet). Fix the questions on Manage Questions. Then Restore it once you're satisfied — since tomorrow hasn't happened yet, it comes back exactly as Upcoming with its original schedule intact.

Editing a Scheduled Exam

What you can change, per state — and why

StateTiming fields
(Date, Start Time, Duration, Prep, Buffer, Allow Manual Submission)		Everything else
(Name, Instructions, Marks, Category, Result Mode, Questions…)
Upcoming (incl. Preparation Available) Editable Editable
Ongoing / Live (incl. buffer) Locked Editable
Completed Locked Editable
Why timing locks once it's no longer Upcoming: once the exam has gone live, some students may already have started — or completed — an attempt built entirely around the original date/time/duration. Silently rewriting that schedule underneath them would make their attempt inconsistent with what they actually experienced. Saving a timing change here is rejected with a message naming the exam's current state; non-timing details save normally.

Manual ⇄ Scheduled mode switch: blocked outright the moment any attempt exists on the exam — Upcoming, Live or Completed, it doesn't matter. This check runs on attempt count, not on the exam's current state, so it applies even to a Scheduled Exam that's still technically Upcoming if a student somehow already has an attempt on it.

Questions are not covered by any of the locks above. Nothing in the current implementation stops you from adding, editing or deleting questions on Manage Questions after students have already attempted the exam — this guide describes what exists today, and that particular guard doesn't exist yet. Changing questions after attempts exist will affect what remaining/future attempts see, while past results stay based on whatever the student actually answered at the time. Use with caution.

Real-World Examples

Five complete scenarios showing how the pieces combine

Example 1 — A strict timed test
Start: 2:00 PM
Duration: 60 min
Prep: 3 min
Buffer: 2 min
Manual Submission: Off

Students can preload from 1:57 PM. The exam is Live from 2:00–3:00 PM. Nobody can submit early — the exam only ends when their own timer reaches zero. Anything already in flight at 3:00 PM is still accepted until 3:02 PM, after which the exam shows Completed.

Example 2 — A relaxed practice exam
Start: 6:00 PM
Duration: 45 min
Prep: 0 min
Manual Submission: On

No preparation window, so the Start button only enables right at 6:00 PM. Students who finish answering early can submit right away instead of waiting out the full 45 minutes — their attempt is finalized the moment they click Submit.

Example 3 — Cancelling an upcoming exam

An exam is scheduled for tomorrow. The admin spots a wrong answer key on Manage Questions today. They click Cancel — the exam vanishes from every student's dashboard immediately, with zero risk since it's still Upcoming and nobody has attempted it. They fix the question, then Restore, and it reappears as Upcoming with the original schedule untouched.

Example 4 — A problem discovered after the exam already started

The exam went Live 10 minutes ago and students are already answering. The admin notices an issue. The exam's timing fields are now locked (see section 9) — the schedule itself can't be changed. Cancel is still available and will stop any new student from starting, but will not interrupt students already answering; they keep going and are auto-finalized at their own deadline as normal.

Example 5 — A student starts late
Start: 2:00 PM
Duration: 30 min

A student opens the instructions page at 2:10 PM and clicks Start. The exam is still within its official window (which stays open until 2:30 PM), so the attempt is created. Their deadline, however, is still 2:30 PM — not 2:40 PM — so they effectively have 20 minutes, not the full 30. See section 4 for the exact rule.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers, grounded in the current implementation

What is the difference between a Manual and a Scheduled Exam?
A Manual Exam is opened and closed by you changing its Status yourself. A Scheduled Exam computes its own status (Upcoming/Ongoing/Completed) purely from the date/time/duration you configured — no manual status changes, ever.
Can I change a Manual Exam into a Scheduled Exam?
Yes, as long as no attempts have been recorded against it yet. Once any attempt exists, the mode is locked either direction.
Can I reschedule an exam after students have attempted it?
No. Once its effective status is no longer Upcoming, its timing fields (date, time, duration, prep window, buffer, manual submission) are locked, whether or not attempts actually exist yet — the lock is based on state, not attempt count.
What happens when the scheduled start time arrives?
The exam's effective status flips to Ongoing across the whole portal (dashboard, notifications, admin list) and the server starts accepting fresh attempts. Nothing needs to be clicked by an admin.
What is Preparation Time?
A window before the official start during which a student can open the instructions page and have their questions/images preloaded, so the exam opens instantly the moment it actually goes live.
Is Preparation Time added to the exam duration?
No. It only affects when a student can start preloading — it has no effect on how long their attempt timer runs once the exam actually starts.
What is the Buffer / Completion Window?
A short grace period after the official end during which a submission already in progress is still accepted by the server, so a moment of network lag right at the deadline doesn't cost a student their attempt.
Does the Buffer increase a student's exam duration?
No. Every student's own timer still reaches zero exactly at the official end. The buffer only changes whether the server keeps accepting the resulting submission after that.
Can students start before the scheduled time?
No — clicking Prepare before the official start only preloads resources and shows a countdown. The actual attempt is only created once the server independently confirms the official start time has arrived.
Can students submit early?
Only if Allow Manual Submission is On for that exam. If it's Off, there is no Submit button, and the server also rejects a manual submit request sent before the deadline.
What happens if a student starts late?
They can start as long as the official window (start → official end) hasn't closed yet, but their deadline is still the official end — they get whatever time is left, never the full duration. See section 4.
What happens if the browser is refreshed during preparation?
Nothing is lost. The server remembers that student already started preparing and resumes them straight back into the countdown (or launches immediately if the start time already passed) instead of making them click Prepare again.
What happens if the student disconnects?
Their answers are saved to the database periodically while they work, not only at final submission. If the deadline arrives while they're disconnected, the server's own background process still finds and finalizes their attempt from those saved answers — it does not require their browser to be present.
What happens when the exam reaches its deadline?
No further attempts can start. Any still-open attempt is automatically finalized by the server, either right away (if the student's browser is present and its timer reaches zero) or shortly after by the background process either way.
What does Cancel do?
Hides the exam entirely from every student (dashboard and notifications) and blocks any new attempt, at any point in its lifecycle. It does not interrupt a student already mid-attempt.
What does Restore do?
Removes the Cancelled marker and lets the exam's status be computed from the clock again, immediately — it does not "un-pause" or shift the original schedule, so if time has already moved on, it may come back as Ongoing or Completed rather than Upcoming.
Can I edit questions after students have attempted the exam?
Yes — the application does not currently block this. It's worth being deliberate about it, since it will affect what remaining or future attempts see while past results stay based on what was actually shown at the time.
Why are some fields locked after the exam is no longer Upcoming?
Because students may already have an attempt built entirely around the original timing — changing the schedule underneath them would make their attempt inconsistent with what they actually experienced.
Can a Completed Scheduled Exam be rescheduled?
No — its timing fields are locked once it's no longer Upcoming, Completed included. If you genuinely need to re-run it, create a new exam.
